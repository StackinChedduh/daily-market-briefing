Session Narrative

What Happened

The morning's tentative risk-off bias was wrong: June CPI fell 0.4% month-over-month (vs. -0.1% consensus), pulling the annual rate down to 3.5% — well below the ~3.9% expected and the largest monthly decline in six years — and that cooling print, not the Hormuz blockade, ended up driving the tape. Rate-hike bets that had been building into the print unwound fast, and chip stocks led a broad relief rally (AMD +5%, Intel +4%, Micron +4.24%, SOXX +4%), flipping Monday's and premarket's chip-sector weakness into the Nasdaq's best day of the window (+0.9%). Big-bank earnings kicked off mixed: JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs rallied hard on strong prints (GS +7.41% was the standout), but Citigroup's blowout beat — $3.15 EPS vs. $2.74 estimated, decade-high revenue, a $30B buyback, and a 12% dividend hike — triggered a sharp sell-the-news reversal (-4.7%), and Wells Fargo fell 3.32% despite the sector's otherwise positive tone, the session's biggest surprise. IBM was the one name where the morning's setup played out as feared: the stock extended Monday's post-close guide-miss reaction to close down nearly 25%, the clearest single-stock casualty of the week. The Iran/Hormuz theme didn't disappear — oil kept climbing into the 4pm ET start of naval blockade enforcement — but it took a back seat to the domestic CPI-and-earnings narrative for the first time in over a week, and Fed Chair Warsh's testimony landed without reportedly derailing the rally.
