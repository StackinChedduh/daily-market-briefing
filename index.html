The Framework — full trading rules reference
1. Trade Setup Criteria (only trade when ALL are met)
  • Time window: 9:45–11:30am or 1:30–3:00pm. Avoid the open (chaotic) and final hour (gamma trap).
  • Clear bias: You need a reason — VWAP direction, prior day level test, macro catalyst. "Feels like it'll go up" doesn't count.
  • VIX check: VIX above 20 = wider spreads, faster moves. Adjust size down. VIX below 15 = sluggish, harder to profit on directional plays.
  • No major news in the next 30 min (Fed speakers, economic data).
2. Position Sizing Rules
  • Max per trade: 3-5% of account (~$300-500 on a $10k account)
  • Max 2 trades open simultaneously
  • Max daily loss: 10% — hit it, you're done for the day, no exceptions
3. Entry
  • Buy slightly OTM (1-2 strikes out), not deep OTM lottery tickets
  • Target options with delta 0.30–0.45 — enough sensitivity without paying for pure gamma
  • Check the bid/ask spread — if it's wider than 20% of the mid price, skip it

Session Narrative

What Happened

This morning's "cautiously constructive but pinned" bias played out almost exactly as described: the cash indices spent the entire session hovering within a few basis points of flat, gyrating between small gains and losses (energy and industrials outperforming, healthcare and consumer names lagging) while everyone waited on the 4 PM bell. July core PCE came in sticky (headline +3.7% YoY, core +3.3% YoY, both a touch hot), but markets shrugged it off once the "core held constant" read gave the Fed room to stay on hold into Friday's Jackson Hole speech. QQQ and SPY both did what the morning's options-positioning read flagged as the bull case — reclaiming and holding their zero-gamma pivots ($710 and $767.52 respectively) — while DKS staged a heavy-volume stabilization bounce (+4.2%) after Tuesday's crash. The real fireworks came after the bell: Nvidia beat on revenue, Data Center growth, and forward guidance by a wide margin (Q3 guide $107-108B vs. $104B consensus) and landed a fresh AWS commitment for 2 million GPUs, yet the stock initially extended its regular-session slide in after-hours trading — a "sell-the-news" pattern that has now shown up after each of Nvidia's last four reports. CrowdStrike delivered the session's cleanest beat-and-raise and was rewarded with an 11.3% after-hours pop; Salesforce's reaction remained unconfirmed at this run's cutoff. Oil extended its decline (WTI -2.6%) on a fresh Iran-Oman revenue-sharing agreement over the Strait of Hormuz, continuing to unwind some of the war-risk premium baked in since the spring.
