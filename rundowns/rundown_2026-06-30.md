## 6. Broader Market Themes

**Sector rotation is real but rate-dependent.** The past week saw DJIA, Russell 2000, and Equal-Weight S&P outperform even as the S&P 500 and Nasdaq fell. Health care, real estate, consumer staples, and industrials led. Schwab's sector outlook rates industrials, materials, health care, and communication services as "most favored" for the next 6-12 months. Consumer discretionary and real estate are least favored. The AI infrastructure build supports industrials and materials as "picks-and-shovels" behind hyperscaler spend.

**Oil and yield interplay:** When yields are below 4.5%, money has rotated to non-tech. When yields rise above 4.5%, capital gravitates back to tech defensives. WTI at $70.67 (-30% Q2) is pulling inflation expectations down, which is supportive for sub-4.5% yields. If oil stabilizes here, the broadening trade has room.

**Tech deleveraging / margin debt risk:** Margin debt hit a record $1.42 trillion in May. Schwab's Peterson flagged that last week's tech selloff may not have fully wrung out speculative positions. Korean memory stocks (Samsung, SK Hynix) dove last week, which typically signals global tech deleveraging. NVDA failed to hold $200 and is down nearly 9% from last week's peak — the definition of a correction.

**AI spending narrative bifurcating:** Hyperscalers (GOOGL, META, MSFT, AMZN) are up ~10% since late March; AI chip makers (NVDA, MU) are up ~60% over the same period. The divergence widens the question: are hyperscalers signaling a slowdown in CapEx growth, or is the selloff overdone? OpenAI IPO delay rumors also weighed on AI sentiment last week.

**SpaceX disruption theme:** The CHTR-SpaceX mobile partnership talks are not a one-day story. Telecom infrastructure is being disrupted — CMCSA spinning out media, Charter pivoting to a SpaceX-powered mobile service. VZ (removed from Dow) is exposed. T and TMUS worth watching for secondary effects.

**Cross-asset:** Gold rebounded above $4,000 today after briefly slipping to $3,987 last Friday (7-month low). Goldman Sachs reaffirmed its $4,900 year-end target. Dollar soft (DXY ~101.26). BTC flat around $60K.
