### Macro Narrative

The dominant story remains Friday's jobs report, even though it landed on a day markets were closed. June nonfarm payrolls rose just 57K against expectations near 110-115K, and April/May were revised down a combined 74K — the labor-force participation rate fell to 61.5%, its lowest since March 2021, with household employment down over 500K. That combination (weak headline, ugly revisions, falling participation) is being read as confirmation the labor market is cooling meaningfully, not just noisily.

For the Fed, this pushes the debate further from "will they hike" toward "when do they cut" — traders have taken a potential near-term hike off the table, with futures pricing patience through the summer. Chair Warsh's Fed, which paused in June citing sticky energy-driven inflation, now has a much harder story to tell if it doesn't at least acknowledge the labor softening in Wednesday's minutes.

Layered on top: last week's semiconductor selloff (AI-capex-ROI anxiety after Citi flagged overcapacity risk and reports of Meta reselling compute capacity) looks to be stabilizing today on sell-side price-target support (Morgan Stanley on LRCX/AMAT/KLAC). Whether that's a genuine reset of the AI-capex debate or a one-week air pocket is the key swing factor for tech leadership this week.
